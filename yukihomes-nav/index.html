What changed, and why

  • One alignment axis on mobile. Every row — link or accordion — shares a left edge, and each chevron sits immediately after its own label. The live build centres two items, left-aligns two, and pins both chevrons to the far right.
  • Submenus use indentation and colour, not lines or boxes. Children sit slightly indented, entering in a short stagger. The parent's divider drops away while open so the pair reads as one block. The live build nests a bordered card under a centred parent — a third alignment system in one menu.
  • The panel is full-bleed on phones. The live overlay is inset, leaving strips of hero video down both edges.
  • Breakpoint moved to 900px. Webflow's 991px default hides every content link on tablet while there's still room for them.
  • Focus is owned, not inherited. A two-tone ring defined in the design system, so it survives dark buttons and video backdrops rather than depending on each browser's default.
  • A scrim behind the header. Gives white text a contrast floor over moving video.
  • Four top-level items. Services and About Us establish the offer, Client Stories proves it, Resources holds supporting material for people who already care. Reached after the main story has done its work.
  • Resources — NipponHomes, Podcast, Blog, Investment Guide, Travel. Off-site destinations carry a label so the menu doesn't mix internal pages and external products without signal.
  • The mobile CTA is pinned to the base of the panel. In thumb reach, and it never scrolls away with both accordions open. It previously sat in the panel header beside the close control, where the destructive action was the easier target.
  • 44px minimum targets on every interactive element.
  • Six type steps as CSS variables, replacing the eleven sizes measured on the live homepage.

Design revisions

  • Hero rebuilt as a type-first editorial layout. The full-bleed Shibuya Crossing video is gone. The hero is now a flat ink-black (#0D0D0D) two-column composition — an oversized three-line headline on the left (~60% width), a single cropped landscape image on the right (~35%), with deliberate negative space around it rather than a background that fills the frame.
  • A new accent colour: acid green (#DFFF00). Used only for the eyebrow rule, the primary hero CTA, hover states, the active-nav underline and one handwritten accent near the image — never as a background or a dominant colour.
  • Body text warmed from white to cream (#F5F1E8). Across the nav, headline and copy, for a softer, editorial-print feel in place of stark white-on-black.
  • Typeface changed to Archivo. A variable grotesque with a width axis, free on Google Fonts. It stays sans serif to keep the studio's action-sports roots and avoid a lux real-estate feel. Helvetica New remains in the stack as the fallback.
  • Expanded, heavier display type in the hero. The headline pushes Archivo to its widest cut (125%) and heaviest weight (800), set in caps with tight tracking and a compressed 0.92 line height — wide letterforms held close together, scaling up to 108px. The nav and wordmark keep the same expanded cut at a much smaller size and weight 700, for a lighter touch than the hero.
  • Hero copy: "Buy Japan. Skip the bullshit." Three short, oversized lines replace the original single-sentence H1, with a small uppercase eyebrow above it ("Real estate for a different kind of life") and a short supporting line below.
  • One editorial image instead of a background video. A single landscape frame — a snowboarder on a run below a snow-capped peak — sits in a plain rectangular crop with small uppercase location and coordinate captions underneath, in the style of a film-still credit line. It's a placeholder graphic; swap in a licensed photograph before shipping (see the caveat below).
  • Nav in the headline face, secondary links and CTAs in Helvetica. Top-level links and mobile menu rows use the same Archivo Expanded Bold as the wordmark. Secondary links in the dropdowns and mobile submenus drop to Helvetica regular, so the hierarchy between parent and child reads at a glance. The CTAs use Helvetica bold in uppercase.
  • All links are cream in every state. Dropdown items, mobile sub-links, external-link labels, chevrons and the footer link are no longer greyed or dimmed. Unselected mobile rows no longer fade when an accordion is open. Hover feedback comes from a background tint or a small shift, not a colour change.
  • Nav moved out of the pill and aligned left. The links sit directly after the logo with no container, and Get Started stays at the far right.
  • Sticky header. The header stays fixed to the top of the screen. After a short scroll its fading scrim is replaced by a solid, blurred background so cream links keep their contrast over any content. The logo, nav and header height stay exactly the same in both states. It also respects the notch and status bar on phones.
  • Logo enlarged by 35%. 65px tall on desktop and 54px on phones, up from 48px and 40px, so the mark holds its own next to the heavier expanded nav type.
  • Two CTA styles, split by role. The nav's "Get Started" stays a small outlined button — transparent with a 0.75px cream stroke, inverting to acid green with ink text on hover. The hero's "Find your dream home" is the bolder move: a solid acid-green rectangle with ink text and 6px corners. Neither uses a pill, gradient or shadow.
  • Buttons use sharp-ish corners. Nav CTAs, dropdown items and the menu button keep the existing 8px radius; the hero CTA drops to 6px, slightly sharper, set from its own radius variable.
  • Tablet menu becomes a drawer. Between 641px and 900px the menu opens as a right-hand drawer capped at 440px over a dimmed backdrop, instead of stretching rows and the CTA across the full screen. Tapping the backdrop closes it. Phones keep the full-screen panel.

Keyboard: Tab through, Enter or Space to open a dropdown, Esc to close. Focus returns to the trigger on close.
