What changed, and why

  • One alignment axis on mobile. Every row — link or accordion — shares a left edge, and each chevron sits immediately after its own label. The live build centres two items, left-aligns two, and pins both chevrons to the far right.
  • Submenus use indentation and colour, not lines or boxes. Children sit slightly indented, entering in a short stagger. The parent's divider drops away while open so the pair reads as one block. The live build nests a bordered card under a centred parent — a third alignment system in one menu.
  • The panel is full-bleed on phones. The live overlay is inset, leaving strips of hero video down both edges.
  • Breakpoint moved to 900px. Webflow's 991px default hides every content link on tablet while there's still room for them.
  • Focus is owned, not inherited. A two-tone ring defined in the design system, so it survives dark buttons and video backdrops rather than depending on each browser's default.
  • A scrim behind the header. Gives white text a contrast floor over moving video.
  • Four top-level items. Services and About Us establish the offer, Client Stories proves it, Resources holds supporting material for people who already care. Reached after the main story has done its work.
  • Resources holds specific destinations, not a repeat of its own label. Journal, Podcast, Investment Guide, Travel Guides — then NipponHomes, set apart by a divider and an external-link arrow rather than mixed in as just another item. The live menu's separate "Search" badge and orphaned Blog slot are gone; nothing in the tray repeats the word "Resources."
  • The mobile CTA is pinned to the base of the panel. In thumb reach, and it never scrolls away with both accordions open. It previously sat in the panel header beside the close control, where the destructive action was the easier target.
  • 44px minimum targets on every interactive element.
  • The burger is pinned to its own grid column, not auto-placed. Below 900px .nav-desktop goes display:none and drops out of the grid entirely, so implicit auto-placement slid .header-actions into the vacated centre track — the burger sat off-centre instead of flush against the right edge. Each header child now gets an explicit grid-column, so the burger stays far-right regardless of which siblings are hidden.
  • Six type steps as CSS variables, replacing the eleven sizes measured on the live homepage.

Design revisions

  • Hero rebuilt as a type-first editorial layout. The full-bleed Shibuya Crossing video is gone. The hero is now a flat ink-black (#0D0D0D) two-column composition — an oversized three-line headline on the left (~60% width), a single cropped landscape image on the right (~35%), with deliberate negative space around it rather than a background that fills the frame.
  • A new accent colour: acid green (#DFFF00). Used only for the eyebrow rule, the primary hero CTA, hover states and the active-nav underline — never as a background or a dominant colour.
  • Body text warmed from white to cream (#F5F1E8). Across the nav, headline and copy, for a softer, editorial-print feel in place of stark white-on-black.
  • Typeface changed to Archivo. A variable grotesque with a width axis, free on Google Fonts. It stays sans serif to keep the studio's action-sports roots and avoid a lux real-estate feel. Helvetica New remains in the stack as the fallback.
  • Expanded, heavier display type in the hero. The headline pushes Archivo to its widest cut (125%) and heaviest weight (800), set in caps with tight tracking and a compressed 0.92 line height — wide letterforms held close together, scaling up to 108px. The nav and wordmark keep the same expanded cut at a much smaller size and weight 700, for a lighter touch than the hero.
  • Hero copy: "Buy Japan. Skip the bullshit." Three short, oversized lines replace the original single-sentence H1, with a small uppercase eyebrow above it ("Real estate for a different kind of life") and a short supporting line below.
  • The collage is dramatically larger — six cards, ~44% of the hero's width. A stable, near-horizontal Niseko photograph (−1°) anchors the composition at roughly 50% of its visual weight; five smaller cards connect to it: a ryokan interior overlapping the upper-right corner, the Shibuya video tucked under the lower-left, a placeholder "snowy village" graphic at the lower-right, a small placeholder "vending machine" detail bridging the two, and a placeholder "train platform" card sitting behind the anchor's upper-left corner — a small peek of depth, not a sixth equal card. Every overlap is controlled (roughly 10–15%), not the heavy 30–40% stacking of an earlier pass. The hero grid's column split widened again, from 1.4fr/1fr to 1.15fr/1fr — imagery now carries close to 45% of the hero's visual weight, up from roughly 25%.
  • The headline scaled down slightly to make room. With the media column wider, the H1's clamp dropped from 6.4vw/max 5.75rem to 5.6vw/max 5rem — enough that "BUY JAPAN." still holds one line. The copy, CTA and structure are untouched.
  • A cinematic photo treatment, not straight source images. Every card — real photo or placeholder — carries a shared filter (desaturated, cooler, slightly more contrast) plus a faint tiled-noise grain, so the collage doesn't read like polished resort-advertising photography.
  • Three labels, not five. Niseko, Tokyo and Hokkaido caption the anchor, Shibuya and snowy-village cards; the interior and lifestyle-detail cards stay unlabelled, so the tags read as occasional captions rather than a chip on every corner.
  • The collage assembles on load, then drifts on scroll. Each card fades and settles into place with a short stagger (the anchor photo first, then the three smaller cards). On scroll, the cards move upward at different rates — the smaller foreground cards drift a little more than the large anchor frame — for a subtle parallax. Both are skipped under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Nav in the headline face, secondary links and CTAs in Helvetica. Top-level links and mobile menu rows use the same Archivo Expanded Bold as the wordmark. Secondary links in the dropdowns and mobile submenus drop to Helvetica regular, so the hierarchy between parent and child reads at a glance. The CTAs use Helvetica bold in uppercase.
  • All links are cream in every state. Dropdown items, mobile sub-links, external-link labels, chevrons and the footer link are no longer greyed or dimmed. Unselected mobile rows no longer fade when an accordion is open. Hover feedback is a background tint or a small shift almost everywhere — the one exception is the Resources dropdown, where hover shifts to acid green (see below).
  • Resources dropdown rebuilt around specific destinations. Journal, Podcast, Investment Guide, Travel Guides, then NipponHomes — set apart at the base by a hairline divider and an external-link arrow (↗) instead of the live site's "Search" badge, and no item repeats the word "Resources." The tray itself is near-black with a hairline border, no card shadow or gradient; items hover to acid green text on a barely-there gray wash, quick (140ms) and understated.
  • Nav moved out of the pill and centered. The links no longer sit in a pill immediately after the logo — the header is now a three-column grid (logo, nav, CTA/burger) with the nav centered in the middle track, logo far left, Get Started and the burger far right.
  • Top nav set in the headline face, all caps. "Services", "Client Stories", "Resources" and "About Us" now match the H1's all-caps treatment, still in Archivo Expanded at nav weight (700) rather than the H1's heavier 800.
  • Sticky header. The header stays fixed to the top of the screen. After a short scroll its fading scrim is replaced by a solid, blurred background so cream links keep their contrast over any content. The logo, nav and header height stay exactly the same in both states. It also respects the notch and status bar on phones.
  • Header slimmed down. Vertical padding cut from 18px to 13px and the logo from 65px (54px on phones) down to a single 50px height at every size, for a lighter bar that still clears the 44px tap target on every control and doesn't shrink further on small screens.
  • Logo swapped for the cream wordmark. The acid-green eye mark now sits in place of the "O" in "HOMES" — the one spot in the nav where the eye motif and the hero's accent colour meet.
  • Two CTA styles, split by role. The nav's "Get Started" stays a small outlined button — transparent with a 0.75px cream stroke, inverting to acid green with ink text on hover. The hero's "Find your dream home" is the bolder move: a solid acid-green rectangle with ink text and 6px corners. Neither uses a pill, gradient or shadow.
  • Buttons use sharp-ish corners. Nav CTAs, dropdown items and the menu button keep the existing 8px radius; the hero CTA drops to 6px, slightly sharper, set from its own radius variable.
  • Tablet menu becomes a drawer. Between 641px and 900px the menu opens as a right-hand drawer capped at 440px over a dimmed backdrop, instead of stretching rows and the CTA across the full screen. Tapping the backdrop closes it. Phones keep the full-screen panel.
  • Footer rebuilt against a seven-point audit. Every header destination now also lives in the footer (Journal, Podcast, Investment Guide, Travel Guides, NipponHomes); it stacks to a strict two-column grid below 700px so no label wraps; the brand column no longer wastes desktop width; Contact holds contact details only, with the rest (About Us, Business Ethics, FAQs, Merch) moved to a new Company group; the logo is the cream wordmark, not the low-contrast navy mark; legal links clear the 44px tap target; and FAQs points at /start#faq instead of the top of checkout. The tel: link matches the displayed number (the old footer dialled a different one).
  • Brand column pared down to logo and tagline. No CTA competing with the header's own "Get Started" — the social icons moved down into Contact, next to the rest of the ways to reach YukiHomes. Column headings pick up the nav's expanded, bolder Archivo cut so the two feel like one type system.

Keyboard: Tab through, Enter or Space to open a dropdown, Esc to close. Focus returns to the trigger on close.
